Crop Insects in Kansas — 41
Corn
Many insects may attack corn at any stage — from seed-infesting all
the way to physiological maturity .  Even as growers wait for plants
to sufficiently dry so harvest can be accomplished,  yield may still
be lost due to prior feeding by stalk-boring insects,  causing plants
to lodge before harvest.  However,  with the widespread use of seed
treatments,  crop rotation,  Bt varieties,  and the understanding of
successful management strategies for most corn pests,  producers can
avoid major losses to insects and have done so relatively successfully
for many years.
Armyworm
Mythimna unipuncta (Haworth)
Larvae attack all types of grass crops
including small grains and corn,  and
when under stress they may feed on
many other plants.  When nearing
maturity ,  armyworm larvae are 1½ to 2
inches long.  Larval head capsules have
honeycomb-like markings,  and the
body lacks obvious hairs.  Overall body
coloration varies,  but is usually greenish-
black with two alternating dark and
orange stripes running lengthwise down
each side plus a light or faint white line on the back.  Larvae pupate
just below the soil surface.  Pupae are dark brown and about ¾ inch
long.  Moths are pale brown to grayish brown with a single small
white spot on each forewing.
Biology: The armyworm is said to
overwinter in a variety of stages
including late instar larvae,  pupae,  and
adults.  In the spring,  adults deposit eggs
where grass is lush,  often in low-lying
areas on wheat or pasture ground.  Field
margins or fields with dense growths
of grassy weeds also are preferred egg-
laying sites.  There can be two to three
generations per year.
