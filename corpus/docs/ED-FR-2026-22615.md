Proposed Changes
We discuss the proposed changes to
the priorities and definitions under the
final notices to which they pertain.
Generally, we do not address proposed
changes that are technical or otherwise
minor in effect.
Proposed Changes From Artificial
Intelligence (AI) to Super Intelligence
(SI)
Current Priorities and Definitions: We
finalized definitions of artificial
intelligence (AI) and AI literacy in the
Secretary’s Supplemental Priority and
Definitions on Advancing Artificial
Intelligence in Education.
Proposed Priorities and Definitions:
We propose, with limited exceptions
described in the next paragraph, to use
the term ‘‘Super Intelligence’’ to replace
‘‘Artificial Intelligence’’ and use ‘‘SI’’ to
replace ‘‘AI’’ in every use of the terms.
This includes the following changes:
(1) proposing to rename the title of the
notice from the ‘‘Notice of Final Priority
and Definitions—Secretary’s
Supplemental Priority and Definitions
on Advancing Artificial Intelligence in
Education’’ to the ‘‘Notice of Final
Priority and Definitions—Secretary’s
Supplemental Priority and Definitions
on Advancing Super Intelligence in
Education.’’
(2) proposing to rename the title of the
final priority from ‘‘Advancing Artificial
Intelligence in Education’’ to
‘‘Advancing Super Intelligence in
Education.’’
(3) proposing to rename the definition
of ‘‘Artificial Intelligence (AI)’’ to
‘‘Super Intelligence (SI).’’
(4) proposing to rename the definition
of ‘‘AI literacy’’ to ‘‘SI literacy.’’
The exceptions to replacing
‘‘Artificial Intelligence’’ with ‘‘Super
Intelligence’’ are in the definition of SI
and in the title of the following
document: ‘‘Department of Labor’s
Artificial Intelligence Literacy
Framework.’’ The definition of SI
includes reference to ‘‘artificial
intelligence’’ therefore, we will continue
to use ‘‘artificial intelligence’’ when
referring to the definition of SI. Later in
this notice we propose to add reference
to a DOL document in the newly titled
Priority on Advancing Super
Intelligence in Education, and we
describe our proposal and our reasons
for doing so. This document and all
references to it, will retain the title of
‘‘Department of Labor’s Artificial
Intelligence Literacy Framework.’’
For the newly titled SI definition, we
propose to adopt the definition of SI
included in President Trump’s
Executive Order (E.O.) 14344
‘‘Inaugurating the Era of Super
Intelligence,’’ dated September 29, 2026,
which says that SI means the
technologies and systems encompassed
by the term ‘‘artificial intelligence’’ as
defined 15 U.S.C. 9401(3). The newly
titled SI literacy definition would be
revised to incorporate the importance of
understanding how SI works and how to
use SI tools effectively and responsibly.
Finally, we propose to add the
definitions of SI and SI literacy to all
uses of the terms in the Secretary’s
Supplemental Priorities on Evidence-
Based Literacy, Educational Choice, and
Returning Education to the States, the
Secretary’s Supplemental Priority on
Meaningful Learning Opportunities, and
the Secretary’s Supplemental Priority on
Career Pathways and Workforce
Readiness.
Reasons: On September 29, 2026, the
President issued E.O. 14434 (91 FR
63129) directing executive departments
and agencies to replace the terms
‘‘Artificial Intelligence’’ and ‘‘AI’’ with
‘‘Super Intelligence’’ and ‘‘SI’’ in official
correspondence, public
communications, policy documents,
and other non-statutory documents. E.O.
14344 explains that this terminology is
intended to reflect the continuously
advancing technological frontier and the
expanding capabilities and applications
of these technologies. Consistent with
this direction, the Department proposes
to update the terms used throughout the
priorities and definitions by replacing
references to ‘‘Artificial Intelligence’’
and ‘‘AI’’ with ‘‘Super Intelligence’’ and
‘‘SI,’’ as discussed previously. These
revisions align the priority with current
Federal terminology and the
Administration’s direction for executive
branch agencies while maintaining the
substantive focus of the priority on
preparing students and educators to
understand, use, and benefit from these
rapidly advancing technologies.
We also propose to adopt the
definition of Super Intelligence from
E.O. 14344. We believe this definition,
as specified in E.O. 14344, is most
appropriate because this definition
provides terminology for implementing
these priorities unless and until it is
superseded by subsequent Presidential
action consistent with applicable law or
by an Act of Congress.
Consistent with this discussion, we
propose to rename the definition of ‘‘AI
literacy’’ to ‘‘SI literacy’’ and to revise
the definition. The current definition of
AI literacy means technical knowledge,
durable skills, civic awareness, and
future ready attitudes, including AI
related ethical reasoning, critical social
inquiry, interdisciplinary problem-
solving, and creativity, required to
thrive in a world influenced by AI. The
definition also indicates that AI literacy
enables learners to engage, create with,
manage, and design AI, while critically
evaluating its benefits, risks, and
implications. In addition to replacing
‘‘AI’’ with ‘‘SI’’, we propose to add to
the definition the importance of
understanding how SI works and how to
use SI tools effectively and responsibly.
We believe the amendment improves
the clarity of the SI literacy definition
and is necessary for promoting the
appropriate integration of SI into
education.
We propose to add the definitions of
‘‘SI’’ and ‘‘SI literacy’’ across all uses of
the terms in the four notices proposed
for revision in this proposed rule. Using
common terminology and definitions
across all of the supplemental priorities
will help ensure consistency in
understanding and implementation in
ED’s grant programs.

subparts. The current part (c) would be

We are proposing to revise the stem for
part (c) to focus on the advancement
and implementation of education choice
options broadly.
Reasons: The Department proposes to
expand this priority to support the
effective implementation of education
choice initiatives, including the new
EFTC. Providing technical assistance
about education services available to
students through scholarships
supported by the EFTC will help ensure
that eligible participants understand the
opportunities available under the tax
credit and how to access and implement
these options. Broadening this priority
will also allow the Department to
support projects that strengthen the
infrastructure necessary to expand
educational freedom, empower families
to make informed decisions, and
increase access to education options
that best meet students’ individual
needs.
Current Priority: The current
Expanding Education Choice priority
does not include options for specific
supports for student parents; talent
marketplaces for postsecondary
enrollment; or children and youth in
foster care or kinship care, or their
caregivers.
Proposed Priority: We propose to add
three new parts to the Expanding
Education Choice priority and
redesignate the current parts as
appropriate. Newly designated part (f)
would focus on expanding access to
childcare and other supports for
student-parents. A second part, newly
designated part (j), would focus on
expanding access to talent marketplaces
and other best practices for
postsecondary enrollment to include
pre-apprenticeships and Registered
Apprenticeships. A third part, newly
designated part (o), would focus on
children and youth in foster care or
kinship care, or their caregivers, by
promoting projects that improve
educational stability and academic
outcomes, supporting caregiver
engagement in the education of children
in their care, and expanding access to
supports across the education spectrum
from early childhood to postsecondary.
Additionally, we propose to redesignate
the other parts of this priority to
conform with the proposed additions
described above.
Reasons: We propose to amend this
priority to ensure that students and
families have access to a broader range
of educational pathways and supports
that allow them to pursue an education
aligned with their unique needs,
circumstances, and career goals. These
additions reflect feedback from our
Federal partners at HHS and DOL,
whose expertise and experience
administering programs serving
children, families, workers, and other
populations helped identify additional
ways in which the priority could more
fully reflect the range of choices and
supports that contribute to educational
access and success.
In particular, HHS highlighted the
importance of recognizing that
meaningful education choice may
depend on whether students and
families can access the supports
necessary to take advantage of those
opportunities. This includes access to
child care and other supports for
student parents, as well as educational
stability, caregiver engagement, and
coordinated supports for children and
youth in foster or kinship care.
Incorporating these areas recognizes that
students’ individual circumstances can
affect their ability to access and persist
in educational opportunities and
provides greater flexibility for ED,
where consistent with applicable
program authorities, to support projects
that address those circumstances across
the education continuum.
DOL similarly brought its expertise in
workforce development and career-
connected learning to identify
opportunities to broaden the priority’s
treatment of postsecondary choice. In
particular, DOL highlighted pre-
apprenticeships and Registered
Apprenticeships as additional pathways
that can expand the range of high-
quality postsecondary education and
training options available to students.
Including these pathways alongside
talent marketplaces and approaches to
postsecondary enrollment better reflects
effective tools that connect jobseekers
with employers and the variety of routes
students may pursue to acquire skills,
credentials, and experience that support
their education and career goals. Taken
together, these changes strengthen the
priority by recognizing that expanding
education choice involves not only
increasing the number or type of
educational options available, but also
helping students and families access
and successfully navigate those options.

Types of Priorities

that meet the priority (34 CFR

Competitive preference priority:
Under a competitive preference priority,
we give competitive preference to an

points, depending on the extent to

an application that meets the priority

75.105(c)(2)(ii)).
Invitational priority: Under an
invitational priority, we are particularly
interested in applications that meet the
priority. However, we do not give an
application that meets the priority a
preference over other applications (34
CFR 75.105(c)(1)).

(ii) Whether or not a study shows a

impact on the validity of the study of

Proposed Definitions for Proposed
Priority on Advancing Super
Intelligence in Education
Computer science means the study of
computers and algorithmic processes,
including their principles, their
hardware and software designs,
theories, computational thinking,
coding, analytics, applications, machine
learning, and super intelligence (SI).
Computer science often includes
computer programming or coding as a
tool to create software, including
applications, games, websites, and tools
to manage or manipulate data; or
development and management of
computer hardware and the other
electronics related to sharing, securing,
and using digital information. In
addition to coding, the expanding field
of computer science emphasizes
computational thinking and
interdisciplinary problem-solving to
equip students with the skills and
abilities necessary to apply computation
to the digital world.
Computer science does not involve
using computers for everyday tasks,
such as browsing the internet or using
tools like word processors,
spreadsheets, or presentation software.
Instead, it focuses on creating and
developing technology, not just utilizing
it.
Super intelligence (SI) means the
technologies and systems encompassed
by the term ‘‘artificial intelligence’’ as
defined in 15 U.S.C. 9401(3).
Super intelligence (SI) literacy means
the technical knowledge, durable skills,
civic awareness and future ready
attitudes, including SI related ethical
reasoning, critical social inquiry,
interdisciplinary problem-solving, and
creativity, required to thrive in a world
influenced by SI. It enables learners to
understand how SI works; use SI tools
effectively and responsibly; engage,
create with, manage, and design SI,
while critically evaluating its benefits,
risks, and implications.
